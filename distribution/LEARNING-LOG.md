## Week 2 — opens Monday 17 August 2026

### Correction — LinkedIn newsletter editions CAN now be scheduled (20 Aug)

Earlier notes (WHEN-YOU-LAND.md, and the implicit assumption in Lesson 5) said
the newsletter was UI-only, manual-publish, "the one thing that can't be
automated." No longer true: the edition publish dialog now carries the same
clock icon as native posts, so an edition can be scheduled to a future instant.
LinkedIn added it since the flight notes were written.

Caveats unchanged and important:
- A scheduled item **cannot be edited** — finish all edits (headings,
  hyperlink, italics) before scheduling; to change it you delete and redo.
- The value of an edition is the subscriber notification **plus** first-90-minute
  comment replies. Scheduling only helps if you'll be online in that window
  anyway — in which case publishing live keeps the edit freedom. Prefer live
  publish; schedule only as a fallback when the slot is unreachable.
- The clock lives on the feed-announcement composer ("Tell your network what
  this edition is about…"), which ships alongside the edition.

## Week 1 — Launch week (10–14 August 2026)

### The numbers

| Metric | Result |
|---|---|
| Newsletter subscribers | 0 → 482 in four days (7.4% of 6,534 followers) |
| Launch-invite conversion | ~3.2% in 24h, ~7.4% by day four — above the typical 1–5% band |
| Post 1 (wrong clock) | 54 impressions in first hours → 700 after rescue |
| Post 2 (right clock) | 352 in 5h, 855 in 24h, 27% out-of-network, no rescue needed |
| Edition 1 article views | ~64 (subscribers largely joined without reading — normal) |
| Comments on launch day | Zero. It did not matter. |
| Post 3 (Fri, third on same theme, no author comment) | 158 impressions, **0% out-of-network** — the week's tuition fee |

### Lesson 1 — Schedule for the audience's clock, not yours

The single biggest factor of the week, worth roughly 10× impressions. LinkedIn
schedules absolute instants: "8am" set from Singapore fires at 1am London and
the algorithm reads the silent first hour as failure. Post 1 (8am SGT = 1am UK)
stalled at 54 impressions; Post 2, identical machinery at 3pm SGT (8am UK),
opened at 6× that pace organically.

The audience map (from post demographics): London 28%, India ~13%, US ~8%,
Sydney 7%, Singapore 3%. **8am UK is the near-optimal single slot** — London's
morning, India's lunch, Sydney's evening, with the US catching the second wave.
Standing rule: while in Singapore, schedule for 3pm SGT; when in London, 8am.

### Lesson 2 — A stalled post can be revived; a mistimed one isn't dead

The "second wind": an author comment with the post's visual, timed to a
UK-waking hour, lifted Post 1 from 54 → 700 impressions (13×) and 18 reactions.
Posts accrue for 24–48 hours; the algorithm re-evaluates on fresh activity.

Standing playbook: **every native post gets an author first-comment** shortly
after firing — the chart/visual where the post references one, a question where
it doesn't. It supplies activity, the missing visual, and a notification hook in
one move.

### Lesson 3 — The launch invite converts silently; don't judge it by noise

Zero comments on launch day felt like failure and was actually a 207-subscriber
day. LinkedIn drips the invite over days rather than blasting it, and people
subscribe from the notification without ever opening the edition. Comments,
reactions and views all lag; the subscriber counter is the only launch metric.

### Lesson 4 — Engagement converts in the comments, not just the feed

The strongest subscriber signal of the week was a substantive comment thread:
a reader announced subscribing *inside* a Post 2 comment, then asked a question
better than the post's own. Replying with a real position (and a question back,
in the commenter's professional lane) is content in its own right — threads
with depth are what push a post out-of-network (17% → 27% over the week).

Also: lead-generation pitches disguised as questions get a gracious reply that
restates the brand in public ("organic by design") — the reply is a billboard,
not a private message.

### Lesson 5 — Mechanics worth remembering

- **Substack Notes cannot be scheduled** — post-now only, so "scheduling" a
  Note means being at the keyboard at 3pm SGT. LinkedIn posts schedule fine
  (clock icon), but a scheduled post cannot be edited — delete and redo.
- **The "link on my profile" close needs a maintained profile**: site link in
  Contact info, newsletter pinned first in Featured (the card shows subscriber
  count — social proof), headline that names the publication. All done; the
  custom "Visit my website" button is Premium-Business-gated and not worth the
  upgrade.
- **Don't Boost.** Paid promotion buys hollow impressions — the "larger hollow
  audience" the brand thesis explicitly rejects.
- **Check live state before trusting checklists**: GSC verification and the
  sitemap had been done since 1 June; the flight-written checklist listed them
  as pending. Probe the dashboard first, assign tasks second.

### Lesson 6 — The funnel's honest arithmetic

Per ~700 impressions: ~3 profile visits, ~1 new follower. Sounds brutal; is
normal. No single post needs to be a hero — the system wins on cadence
(3 posts + 2 Notes a week, every week) times conversion at the profile
(headline → Featured → subscribe). Site traffic is deliberately the lagging
metric: attention → owned audience → recurring per-essay traffic → search.

### Lesson 7 — Two servings of one idea per week, not three (learnt 15 Aug)

Post 3 — the third post on the same essay in five days, fired on a Friday
without its author comment — managed 158 impressions and 0% out-of-network.
Three causes stacked: theme fatigue (the network had seen the curve twice),
the Friday launch pad (the 24–48h accrual window dies into the weekend), and
no first-hour author activity (now proven to be the difference between 0% and
27% out-of-network on our own data).

**Operating change from week 2:**
- **Two native posts per essay in launch week** — the two strongest angles,
  Mon and Wed. The pack still holds three; the third becomes a **reserve
  asset**, fired weeks later on a news peg or in back-catalogue rotation,
  when it reads as fresh rather than repeated.
- **The author comment is non-negotiable and immediate** — visual or
  question, posted within minutes of the post going live, never deferred.
- **Friday changes job:** carousel or newsletter edition on the fortnights
  one is due; otherwise nothing. A two-post week is a full week.
