In 1450, copying a single Bible by hand took a scribe about a year.

By 1500 — fifty years — scribes had gone from producing nearly 100% of Europe's books to under 2%. An entire profession, gone in a generation.

I keep a chart of this on my wall. It's the number of books in the world from 1450 to today, and it starts curving upward the moment Gutenberg combined a wine press with movable type. It has never stopped.

Here's what unsettles me about it.

The people living through that first jump felt exactly what we feel now. They had fake news — sensationalist pamphlets that triggered real social unrest. They had job displacement — those scribes. They had a truth crisis — when anyone can print, who's the expert?

And on the other side of all that chaos was the Enlightenment. The Scientific Revolution happened because, for the first time, scientists could build on each other's work instead of starting from scratch.

The printing press broke the bottleneck on distributing ideas. AI is the printing press for the second bottleneck — generating them. It didn't arrive to replace your thinking. It arrived to scale your intent.

The press is running. The only question that's ever mattered at a moment like this is what you're going to say with it.
