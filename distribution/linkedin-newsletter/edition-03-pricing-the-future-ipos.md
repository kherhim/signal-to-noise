Three of the largest IPOs in history are lining up for 2026: SpaceX, Anthropic,
OpenAI. Most of the coverage is about the valuations. From a CFO's chair, that's
the least interesting thing about them.

These offerings are less about hype and more about how public capital, governance
and risk will reshape three very different — but tightly interlinked — business
models. Read them through three lenses.

## Capital intensity

All three burn enormous capital, but differently. SpaceX is launch and satellite
infrastructure — a vertically integrated connectivity business with quasi-utility
characteristics, anchored in Starlink's recurring revenue. Anthropic and OpenAI
are frontier-model training cycles: large, lumpy capex, with the investor question
being whether efficiency improves as models are reused across products. In every
case the question isn't "how big is the raise" — it's what free-cash-flow
trajectory justifies the multiple.

## Governance complexity

Founder control and potential dual-class structures at SpaceX, with
national-security entanglements attached. Safety-oriented, multi-stakeholder
governance at Anthropic. And OpenAI's genuinely unusual path — non-profit to
capped-profit to conventional public company, with board history and legal
overhangs in the mix. Governance isn't a soft factor here. It drives index
eligibility, stewardship debates, and ultimately the cost of capital.

## Partner dependency

This is the lens that gets underweighted, and it may matter most. Anthropic leans
on hyperscaler capital *and* infrastructure. OpenAI is deeply integrated —
economically and technically — with a key strategic partner. The prospectus
question is brutal and simple: where does the economic value actually accrue? At
the foundation-model layer, inside the cloud partnership, or in downstream
enterprise contracts? If it accrues to the partner, public investors are
underwriting someone else's moat.

## What matters most

| Company | Capital intensity | Governance | Partner dependency |
|---|---|---|---|
| SpaceX | Very high — launch + satellite capex | High — founder control, national-security | Moderate — vertically integrated |
| Anthropic | High — frontier training + safety research | Medium-high — multi-stakeholder | High — hyperscaler capital + infra |
| OpenAI | High — training cycles + infra | High — capped-profit legacy, legal overhangs | Very high — deep partner integration |

These deals are a test case for how public markets price things that sit somewhere
between infrastructure, software, and regulated utility. Whatever numbers they
land on will ripple across private AI and space valuations, secondaries, and the
cost of capital for every smaller firm hoping to follow.

Buying the hype is easy. Reading the cap table is the job.
