# Signal to Noise, Edition 4

**Title:** The landlord finances the tenant

Cover: the essay's OG image. Freshness verified 23 Sep: the Reuters report of 11 Sep remains the most recent update on the Nvidia talks.*

Anthropic is expected to float before the US midterms in November, with Nvidia discussing an anchor investment. Coverage largely concentrates on the cheque's size. That cheque also changes what revenue tells us.

Nvidia is weighing an investment of up to $10bn in Anthropic's flotation, while Anthropic hopes to raise up to $100bn. Using those reported upper limits, the chipmaker would provide roughly a tenth of its customer's listing proceeds. [Reuters reported](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/nvidia-in-talks-to-invest-up-to-10-billion-in-anthropic-ipo--reuters-4898582) the talks in mid-September, with a valuation near $2 trillion. Reuters cautioned that both the plans and the timetable could change. That caveat covers all the figures below.

An anchor investor that also supplies compute to its customer has two interests in the flotation. Money invested in equity can flow back as sales. When it does, those sales no longer establish that somebody beyond the arrangement wanted to buy the product. I want to examine one ratio, which I call the outside share. What percentage of booked revenue drew on money that started somewhere other than your company?

## The shape of the loop

The companies announced the arrangement themselves. [Anthropic's release of 18 November 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/news/microsoft-nvidia-anthropic-announce-strategic-partnerships) says that "NVIDIA and Microsoft are committing to invest up to $10 billion and up to $5 billion respectively in Anthropic", and that "Anthropic has committed to purchase $30 billion of Azure compute capacity".

The cash travels through successive steps. Nvidia funds Anthropic. Anthropic buys Azure capacity from Microsoft. Microsoft purchases systems from Nvidia, which books the proceeds as data centre revenue. No individual contract requires Nvidia to pay Anthropic and Anthropic to pay Nvidia, so the overall pattern is hard to recognise. Microsoft occupies the middle, and each signatory sees an agreement that looks non-circular. The upper limits stand alongside each other, up to $15bn in equity commitments against up to $30bn in purchase commitments. The money's indirect path hides the loop; it leaves the loop intact.

## What the balance sheet has started to show

Accounting rules do not oblige companies to report the revenue they have financed. Nvidia publishes its equity investments in surrounding businesses instead. The [results for the quarter ended 26 July 2026](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-second-quarter-fiscal-2027) report $42,783m in marketable equity securities against $12,886m six months before, plus $51,157m in non-marketable securities against $22,251m. Across half a year, those two balances together increased from about $35bn to about $94bn. Quarterly sales totalled $96.2bn, leaving the equity portfolio's carrying value equivalent to roughly a quarter's revenue.

Bank of America's Vivek Arya regards the [$70 billion committed across ecosystem partners](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/article/nvidias-70-billion-bet-on-openai-anthropic-and-others-could-pay-off-big-for-shareholders-150039868.html) as comfortably affordable. He is right on affordability. That was never the concern. A business can fund demand for its products over several years, honour every cheque and still carry the wrong valuation. Investors misprice it when they mistake what its sales demonstrate.

## Lucent already ran this experiment

The filings from the previous large-scale version of this experiment are still available. Lucent Technologies financed customers' purchases of its equipment through loans. Its [10-Q/A for the quarter ended 31 December 2000](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001006240/000095011701500564/a29849.txt) reports that "As of December 31, 2000, Lucent had made commitments or entered into agreements to extend credit to certain customers for an aggregate of approximately $5.7 billion", of which "approximately $1.8 billion had been advanced and was outstanding". Revenue for the quarter fell from $8,065m to $5,841m, partly because of "a more selective vendor financing program".

The supplier reduced its financing, and its revenue declined. During the quarter when funding ceased, the seller reported a measure of the loop from within the arrangement.

The current version is less visible. Credit involves unforgiving accounting. When a customer cannot pay, the resulting provision is visible to analysts. Equity produces no provision. The carrying value merely rises alongside the sector, before a delayed write-down arrives in one hit.

## The outside share

The same test applies to any company that funds its own customers. What proportion of this period's booked revenue came from customers able to pay without capital from you? Hardly anyone calculates that figure, since disclosure remains optional. Four questions cover it.

- **Exposure by counterparty.** For each of the twenty largest customers, what equity, credit, guarantee or purchase commitment have we extended, and what did they spend with us this year? Those columns are rarely kept together.
- **Cohort behaviour.** Do funded customers grow their spend faster than unfunded ones? If so, part of the growth rate you are quoting is a financing rate.
- **The stop test.** If we wrote no more cheques from tomorrow, what happens to next year's revenue? Lucent answered that one by accident.
- **Where the number lands.** Is the funded cohort's growth inside the figure the market is capitalising? A multiple applied to loop-funded revenue capitalises your own capital twice.

A business can do all of this without a trillion-dollar balance sheet. Holding a minority interest in a distributor and booking sales through it brings some revenue into a loop. No reporting line labelled *revenue we funded* will ever expose that connection. In [The cognitive supply chain](https://signal-to-noise.co/insights/the-cognitive-supply-chain/?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=the-landlord-finances-the-tenant) I made the case for that discipline when assessing suppliers. Customers require the same scrutiny.

## What is not disclosed

We still lack two answers. First, we do not know whether this investment represents fresh money. Nvidia had committed "up to $10 billion" back in November 2025. Nothing I have seen confirms whether the proposed investment reshapes the earlier commitment. Second, we do not know how large the loop is. Anthropic's annualised run rate passed $65 billion by the end of July, rising from roughly $9 billion at the end of 2025. Someone outside the business cannot work out the proportion supplied by customers that compute sellers had themselves financed.

Neither uncertainty constitutes an accusation. A buyer considering a valuation near $2 trillion lacks the two most necessary figures, and therefore prices the loop alongside demand using a single multiple. In [Pricing the future](https://signal-to-noise.co/insights/pricing-the-future-spacex-anthropic-openai-ipos/?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=the-landlord-finances-the-tenant) I examined the partner as a source of concentration risk for its customer. With an anchor investor, the relationship runs in reverse. The partner backs the flotation and books the sales, bringing dependency into the valuation calculation.

A tenth attracts the headlines, but debating that figure misses the relevant measure. Nobody has yet published the share that matters. The first business to disclose it will do its shareholders a substantial service.
