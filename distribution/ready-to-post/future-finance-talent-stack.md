# Ready to post — The future finance talent stack

*Canonical: https://signal-to-noise.co/insights/future-finance-talent-stack/*

This one carries a claim built to be argued with: the best finance teams will look
less like finance teams. Finance leaders and recruiters have skin in that fight — some
will nod, some will bristle, and both will comment. Lead with the shift from processing
engine to decision engine, not with an announcement. Finished posts below. No link in
any body.

---

## LinkedIn — Post 1 (the flagship — lead with the claim)

> The next great finance hire may not look like a traditional finance hire at all.
>
> For years, finance ran on a stable bargain: produce reliable reporting, hold control, support planning, keep the organisation out of trouble. That still matters. It no longer defines the whole job.
>
> The function is moving from a processing engine to a decision engine. Finance leaders are now expected to support growth strategy, AI deployment, cost transformation, scenario planning and enterprise-wide decisions — not just to report what happened, but to interpret what is changing and shape what should happen next.
>
> That changes the profile of talent you need. A team built for accuracy and control is not automatically built for speed, judgement and influence. Yet many functions are still hiring for the first set while being judged on the second.
>
> The shift here is not just technological. It is occupational. The strongest teams of the next few years will be built around hybrids — people who can operate between systems, data and decisions. Commercially literate. Data fluent. Operationally credible. Comfortable turning ambiguity into action.
>
> That is a very different shape from the classic finance pyramid. And it is where the real talent battle is going to be fought.
>
> (Full essay on my site — link on my profile.)

**Why this works:** opens with the provocative claim, then earns it fast with the processing-engine/decision-engine turn. The "hiring for the first, judged on the second" line is the one people quote back. No link tax.

## LinkedIn — Post 2 (the AI-raises-judgement angle — most contrarian)

> There is a simplistic view that AI will mainly shrink finance teams. Something more interesting is likely to happen.
>
> AI will automate parts of the work, speed up close cycles, improve forecasting support. All true. But as automation expands, human value shifts upwards — it does not disappear.
>
> The scarcest capability becomes judgement. Deciding whether the data is sound. Whether the output makes sense. Whether the model is being used in the right context. And what the business should actually do with the answer.
>
> So the future talent stack is not humans plus AI. It is humans whose judgement becomes *more* valuable because AI has taken away some of the mechanical work.
>
> That reframes the hiring question entirely. If judgement is the scarce input, you are no longer optimising a team for throughput. You are building for people who can tell a plausible answer from a trustworthy one — and stand behind the difference.
>
> A team built purely for efficiency will not clear that bar. The premium has moved to the part of the job a model cannot hold.
>
> (Full essay on my site — link on my profile.)

**Why this works:** takes the popular "AI shrinks finance" take and inverts it. The plausible-vs-trustworthy line gives recruiters and CFOs something concrete to disagree about.

## LinkedIn — Post 3 (the CFO-as-architect angle)

> The CFO role is about to change more than the finance team's.
>
> For a long time, finance leaders acted as talent consumers — hiring familiar profiles into familiar roles. That is no longer enough. The job now is talent architect.
>
> That means building a function around capability mixes, not just job titles. Deciding where to hire externally, where to upskill internally, and where to redesign the work altogether. And creating career paths for people who do not fit the old mould but may be exactly what the future function needs.
>
> Because the divide over the next few years will not simply be between teams that use AI and teams that do not. It will be between finance teams that adapt their identity and those that cling to an outdated one.
>
> The best finance teams will not look like more efficient versions of the old model. They will look like something different altogether — core specialists, translators who connect finance to the rest of the business, builders who understand systems and data, operators who use finance to drive action.
>
> More efficient is not the goal. Different is.
>
> (Full essay on my site — link on my profile.)

**Why this works:** "talent consumer vs talent architect" is a clean, restack-able reframe aimed squarely at the CFO reading it. Ends on a short, quotable contrast.
