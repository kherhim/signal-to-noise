## LinkedIn — Post 1 (the framing post — post now)

> Three of the largest IPOs in history are lining up for 2026: SpaceX, Anthropic, OpenAI. Most of the coverage is about the valuations. As a CFO, I think that's the least interesting thing about them.
>
> Read them through three lenses instead of the hype:
>
> **Capital intensity.** All three burn enormous capital — launch and satellite infrastructure, frontier-model training cycles. The question isn't "how big is the raise," it's "what's the free-cash-flow trajectory that justifies the multiple."
>
> **Governance complexity.** Founder control and dual-class structures at SpaceX. Safety-oriented, multi-stakeholder governance at Anthropic. OpenAI's strange journey from non-profit to capped-profit to public company, with legal overhangs attached. Governance drives index eligibility and, eventually, cost of capital.
>
> **Partner dependency.** This is the one that gets underweighted. Anthropic leans on hyperscaler capital and infrastructure. OpenAI is deeply integrated with a single strategic partner. The prospectus question is brutal and simple: is this an independent company, or an R&D arm of someone bigger?
>
> These three deals are a test case for how public markets price things that sit somewhere between infrastructure, software, and regulated utility. Whatever number they land on will ripple through every private AI and space valuation behind them.
>
> Buying the hype is easy. Reading the cap table is the job.
>
> (I broke down all three, side by side, on my site — link on my profile.)

**Why this works:** authoritative, structured, and it reframes a hype story into a rigour story — which is exactly the positioning ("signal, not noise").

## LinkedIn — Post 2 (the single-question post — most shareable)

> One question separates a real AI platform from an expensive R&D lab:
>
> **Where does the economic value actually accrue?**
>
> At the foundation-model layer? Inside the cloud partnership? In downstream enterprise contracts?
>
> For a company raising at a 300-billion-plus valuation while depending on a hyperscaler for both its capital *and* its infrastructure, that's not a philosophical question. It's the whole prospectus.
>
> If the value accrues to the partner, you're underwriting someone else's moat.
>
> It's the test I'd apply to every AI company heading for public markets in 2026 — and to a lot of the private ones too.
