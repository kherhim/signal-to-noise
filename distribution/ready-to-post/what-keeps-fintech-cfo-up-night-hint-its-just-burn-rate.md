## LinkedIn — Post 2 (the "not just burn rate" reframe)

> "What's your runway?"
>
> It's the first question every fintech investor asks. It's also the least interesting one.
>
> Burn rate matters. But in fintech it's not just how fast you're spending — it's how fast capital markets shift. One quarter you're the darling of VCs. The next, sentiment turns and you're suddenly in cash-conservation mode, with the same spreadsheet and a very different mood.
>
> Now layer on interest-rate volatility, token-market disruption for the crypto players, and bank-partnership risk. Cash planning stops being a quarterly ritual and becomes a daily exercise in scenario modelling.
>
> This is why the runway metaphor is incomplete. Fintech CFOs don't just track the runway. We obsess over weather conditions on the landing strip — because the length of the strip is fixed, and the weather is what actually decides whether you land.
>
> The number on the deck is noise. The sensitivity of that number to a market that can flip in a quarter — that's the signal.
>
> (Full breakdown of what keeps fintech CFOs up — on my site, profile link.)

**Why this works:** takes the one question everyone asks and reframes it as the wrong question. The "weather on the landing strip" image is the piece's best line and lands hard with anyone who has lived a funding round.

## LinkedIn — Post 3 (the AI / speed-without-control angle)

> We've sped up every decision in the business. Are we sure we've made them better?
>
> That's the 2am thought I keep coming back to as a fintech CFO.
>
> We all want it — faster reconciliation, smarter risk decisions, real-time anomaly detection. AI promises all of that and more. But every tool we bolt on adds complexity, and complexity brings its own risks: explainability gaps, regulatory grey zones, and a quiet dependence on black-box models we can't fully see into.
>
> The pressure to automate is real. So is the pressure to understand what's happening under the hood. Those two pressures pull in opposite directions, and the CFO sits exactly where they meet.
>
> Speed isn't the win. A faster wrong decision is still a wrong decision — it just arrives sooner and scales further before anyone notices.
>
> Automate, yes. But keep asking the unfashionable question: not "is it quicker?" but "is it better, and can we explain why?"
>
> (The rest — fraud, regulation, runway, talent — is on my site. Link on my profile.)

**Why this works:** the opening question is a genuine leadership worry, not a hot take. It flatters the reader's caution and gives them a line to reuse in their own AI conversations.

**Note 2** — the trust line
> "No fintech grows faster than trust can be lost."
>
> One breach doesn't just hit the P&L. It hits valuation, investor confidence and customer stickiness — all in the same night. That's why fraud, not spend, is the thing that actually wakes finance leaders up.

**Note 3** — the AI question (question form, invites replies)
> Honest question for fellow fintech leaders: we've sped up every decision in the business — reconciliation, risk scoring, anomaly detection. Are we sure we've made them better?
>
> Or have we just bought faster answers from models we can't fully explain?

**Note 4** — the landing-strip line
> Fintech CFOs don't just track the runway. We obsess over weather conditions on the landing strip.
>
> Burn rate tells you the length of the strip. It tells you nothing about capital markets flipping from "darling of VCs" to "cash-conservation mode" in a single quarter. The second one is what actually lands the plane.

**Note 5** — the culture line + take
> "Culture isn't soft stuff. In fintech, it's survival infrastructure."
>
> Fintech attracts brilliant, ambitious people and then burns them out — hybrid models, sky-high expectations, "scale now, fix later." The finance team you're building for what's next is the one asset that doesn't show up on the runway chart.
