# Essay audio (S13): design

Date: 27 October 2026. Roadmap item S13. Status: draft for the owner's approval. Builder check done; the owner answered both open questions on 27 October.

## Intent

Readers can listen to every essay on signal-to-noise.co. A player sits under the essay title; it reads the published text aloud in a synthetic voice, labelled as AI-narrated.

Why: some readers will listen when they would not read (commute, gym). The owner tested three voices on 27 October. The cloned voice of the author was dropped. Kokoro's British voice (Lewis) was "really bad". Chatterbox's built-in American voice was "much better" and is the one chosen.

## What the owner decided

- **Voice:** Chatterbox (resemble-ai/chatterbox, MIT licence), its built-in default voice, running on the owner's Mac. No paid service, no account.
- **Coverage:** every published essay, old and new. The back catalogue is narrated once (about 66 essays, roughly 9 to 11 hours of Mac time, run overnight).
- **Timing for new essays:** the evening before publish. Once the essay is final and gated, the Mac narrates it (about 10 minutes) and the audio ships with the Wednesday publish. If the text changes afterwards, the audio is remade.
- **Label:** a pill on the player reading "AI-narrated", with the tooltip "Read aloud by a synthetic voice generated by AI from the published text." This is the one place the site says AI made something; the owner chose it. The rule against disclosing AI authorship of the writing is unchanged.
- **Podcast:** not in this build. A podcast feed (Apple Podcasts, Spotify, RSS) becomes its own roadmap item once audio has run for a few weeks. The player shows no podcast links.
- **Player design:** as approved in the mockup and seen in the spike: play button, "Listen to this essay · N min", the label, back 15 / forward 15, speed (1, 1.25, 1.5, 0.75), a waveform of the real audio with section ticks, a collapsible Sections list that jumps to each heading, the heading being read highlighted, and a slim bar fixed to the bottom of the screen once the player scrolls away.

## Goals

1. Every published essay page has a working player with correct audio.
2. Audio never goes stale: if the essay text changes, the build knows and says so.
3. Narrating a new essay is one command, and a scheduled job does it the evening before publish without anyone asking.
4. Nothing new costs money.

## Non-goals

- Podcast feed (later item).
- The author's own or cloned voice.
- A choice of voices for readers.
- Narrating letters, Buffett pages, trackers or the homepage.
- Reading charts, tables, image captions, source links or the methodology note.

## Anti-goals (failure even if it works)

- Audio that says something different from the page, such as an old version of the text, or a figure read wrongly.
- Audio files in the public git repo (they would add hundreds of megabytes of history for ever).
- Making every site deploy slow, for example pushing 300 MB of audio through every `./deploy.sh`.
- Any automation that publishes or changes the live site by itself. The scheduled job only makes files; deploying stays the normal merge-then-deploy step.
- An essay page that breaks or shows an empty player when its audio is missing. No audio means no player.

## How it works

### What gets read

The narration script is built from the essay's Markdown body, the same text readers see:

- Kept: paragraphs, headings (each heading starts a section), block quotes and list items.
- Dropped: `<figure>` and `<details>` blocks, tables, images, HTML, link targets, "(Source)" links and the methodology paragraph.
- Spoken forms: `$13.5 billion` is read as "13.5 billion dollars", `$2.68` as "2 dollars 68", `22%` as "22 per cent", `S&P` as "S and P", `3–4%` as "3 to 4 per cent". The spike's rules are the starting point.
- A small pronunciation file in the repo maps words Chatterbox gets wrong to spellings it says right. It starts empty, and entries are added only when the owner or a listen finds a word it gets wrong.

Text is spoken one sentence at a time and joined with short silences (0.25 s between sentences, 0.55 s between paragraphs, 0.8 s before a heading). The spike did the same, and the owner liked the result.

### What gets stored

For each essay `<slug>`:

- `src/data/audio/<slug>.json` (committed): duration, sections (heading text and start time), 200 waveform peaks, the voice and engine version, and a hash of the narration script it was made from.
- `<slug>.m4a` (AAC, mono, 64 kbps, about 5 MB for 10 minutes): **not committed**. It is kept on the Mac in an audio folder outside the repo and uploaded to the server separately.

### Staleness check (owner's decision: hide the player)

At build time the site recomputes each essay's narration-script hash and compares it with the stored one. On a mismatch, the build prints a warning naming the essay and **leaves that essay's player off the page** until fresh audio exists, so readers never hear words that differ from the page. The build still succeeds, so a typo fix never blocks a deploy. On publish day, any edit made after Tuesday's narration is remade (about 10 minutes) before the deploy. The Tuesday job catches any other mismatches.

### Checking the audio is right (owner's decision: automatic check plus spot checks)

- **Automatic:** after narrating, a free speech-to-text model on the Mac (Whisper, run locally) transcribes each file and compares the transcript with the narration script.
  - A sentence that doesn't match is remade, up to a few times.
  - If it still fails, the essay is flagged, and no audio ships for it. Its page has no player until it passes.
  - Figures are compared in their spoken form.
  - The match threshold is set during the build by testing it on known-good and deliberately broken audio.
- **Spot checks:** before the back catalogue goes live, the owner listens to 2 or 3 essays the builder chooses: one figure-heavy, one with many names, and one long. Any word Chatterbox says wrong goes in the pronunciation file and those essays are remade.

### Serving the audio

Each m4a gets a content-hash filename (for example `<slug>.<hash8>.m4a`, recorded in the JSON). That way a remade file has a new URL and Cloudflare's long cache never serves an old copy. Uploads use the same gcloud IAP route as `deploy.sh`.


The audio lives on the existing web server in its own folder, outside the folder that `./deploy.sh` replaces, and nginx serves it at `/audio/`. Audio uploads go through their own small script that only adds or replaces files. That keeps site deploys fast, and the deploy's `--delete` can never remove audio. The nginx change goes in `infra/nginx-signal-to-noise.conf` like the other server settings. Cloudflare caches the files like the images.

### Making the audio

- A script in the repo narrates one essay, a list of essays, or everything missing or stale. It writes the JSON into the repo and the m4a into the audio folder, then uploads new or changed m4a files.
- Chatterbox runs in its own Python environment on the Mac, set up by a one-time script (Python 3.11, `chatterbox-tts`, and `setuptools<81`, which Chatterbox's watermarker needs).
- Chatterbox adds its inaudible AI watermark to every file. That is kept; it fits the label.

### The evening-before job

A scheduled job on the Mac runs every Tuesday at 19:00, like the existing Monday jobs. It handles two kinds of essay differently:

- **Published essays with missing or stale audio:** narrated, checked, uploaded, and their JSON files sent in one pull request.
- **Wednesday's essay:** the draft dated the next day in the working copy. Until it is published, its audio stays on the Mac, so nothing about an unpublished essay reaches the server or the public repo early. The job narrates and checks it, then leaves the m4a and JSON locally.
  - **Who does the next step:** since the essay line was paused, publishing is done by hand in a session (move the draft live, PR, merge, deploy).
  - **Upload and commit:** that publish step uploads the m4a and commits the JSON with the essay, and remakes the audio first if the text changed after Tuesday.

If the job fails, the essay publishes without a player and the owner is told, with no silent gaps.

(Builder's call, from review: draft or unpublished audio is never uploaded or committed before publish.)

### The back catalogue

A one-off overnight run narrates all published essays and uploads them, then one PR adds all the JSON files. Drafts (`draft: true`) are never narrated.

### Page changes

- The player component goes on the essay page between the header and the text, as in the spike, and only when that essay's JSON exists.
- The player is left out of site search.
- Essay tiles and lists do not change.

## Constraints

- British English in every shipped string. The label and tooltip go through the full pre-publish gate (plagiarism, BrE, Layer A, Layer B).
- PRs only, merged with merge commits. Deploy after every merge.
- The draft essay must never be narrated or uploaded.
- No secrets or audio in the public repo.
